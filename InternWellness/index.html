HELLO, Welcome!

Before you enter into the main page, meet Jamyang: the intern behind this project

1. Introduction

My name is Jamyang Chozom, and I have done my six month professional internship at Bhutan Data Scientist Pvt. Ltd. At the beginning of my internship, my project focused on the integration of Artificial Intelligence in credit default analysis. I was excited to work on a technical topic and learn new things and explore beyond what the classroom offered.

2. Initial Internship Experience

As my internship progressed, I began to notice something beyond technical work. Many interns, including myself, were experiencing stress. I was also going through similar challenges. Managing responsibilities alongside personal commitments became increasingly difficult, leading to missed deadlines and additional pressure.

3. Realization

This became a turning point for me. I realized that I did not just want to overcome stress, but also use it as an opportunity for growth. I also wanted to support others going through similar experiences. Stress began to feel less like an individual problem and more like a shared issue.

4. Change of Project Focus

I decided to shift my project from credit default analysis to intern stress and wellbeing. This felt more meaningful, and relevant to myself and to my fellow interns. That experience of distress, facing challenges, feelings of giving up led to the creation of a platform designed to support interns in managing stress and improving wellbeing.

5. Solution Development

Towards the end of my project findings, I was wondering what it would be like if I created a safe virtual platform that was designed not only for interns but to all the people out there dealing with the stress of daily life. Thus as an extension of my small work, I decided to transform lived experience into practical support. This website will try to provide simple tools and resources such as personalised chatbot, relaxation techniques, breathing exercises, fun games, and guided content to help interns not only manage stress in their daily lives but also use stress for their personal growth.

6. Scope and Impact

Although data collected are from lived experiences of Gedu College of Business Studies' interns, the challenges addressed are universal. Stress, burnout, and wellbeing are common among professional settings, making this solution applicable beyond geographical boundaries.

7. Learning Outcome

This journey helped me understand people, their struggles, and how data and technology can be used to create meaningful impact. I gained practical skills and a deeper appreciation of human-centered problem solving.

Key Findings
The top stressors among GCBS interns were time management with 41.98% , followed by misalignment between tasks and course of study with 37.04%, being unable to meet supervisor's expectations, difficulty adjusting to professional environments, and balancing personal responsibilities alongside work commitments.
Most interns experienced moderate stress, some interns sometimes felt emotionally exhausted, frustrated, or tensed about their workload, suggesting moderate stress levels. But they also reported personal growth such as being excited to learn more, feeling proud after completing difficult tasks and improved focus. Overall, while stress is often viewed negatively, the findings indicate that it can also contribute to motivation, learning, and personal development when managed effectively.
Common coping methods included calling family and friends, ignoring the problem, doing physical exercise and seeking advice from supervisors.
Despite high stress levels, most interns reported personal growth. Stress, when managed, can help in improving focus and productivity. However, unmanaged stress can lead to burnout and decreased performance. The findings suggest that while stress is common, it can have both positive and negative outcomes depending on how it is managed.
Institutions could implement structured wellbeing check-ins and peer support groups, friendly working environments, and asigning manageable workloads. Addressing interns' wellbeing and health leads to better performance and stronger professional development.
